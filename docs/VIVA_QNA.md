# WiMotion 2.0 — Final Viva Q&A Guide (Examiner Preparation)

This guide contains the most probable questions examiners will ask during project evaluation, paired with clear, confident, scientifically grounded answers in easy English.

---

### Q1: What is Channel State Information (CSI), and how is it different from RSSI?
**Answer:**
> "RSSI (Received Signal Strength Indicator) is just a single macroscopic scalar value representing overall packet power. It fluctuates violently due to multi-path fading and cannot detect fine-grained movement.  
> CSI (Channel State Information), on the other hand, reveals subcarrier-level channel frequency response across 64 orthogonal OFDM subcarriers (HT20 mode). When a human moves through the Fresnel zones between transceivers, body reflection alters the amplitude and phase of specific subcarriers. This microscopic fingerprint allows us to detect human presence, motion, and spatial sector without wearables or cameras."

---

### Q2: What is the hardware topology of WiMotion 2.0?
**Answer:**
> "Theoretically, WiMotion 2.0 specifies an **8-link distributed sensing mesh** formed by:
> - **Two Receivers (RX1 and RX2)**: ESP32 development boards listening in CSI promiscuous monitor mode at 921,600 baud (`COM3` Master AP, `COM8` Passive Sniffer).
> - **Four Transmitter Beacons (TX1, TX2, TX3, TX4)**: Positioned in four distinct room sectors (TX1=NE/Z1, TX2=NW/Z2, TX3=SW/Z3, TX4=SE/Z4).
> In our **physical laboratory kit**, we currently possess **4 physical COTS ESP32 microcontrollers** (deployed as 1 Master RX + 3 TX Beacons, or 2 RX + 2 TX). The software and telemetry pipeline are fully designed to support the full 6-node / 8-link mesh while gracefully operating over 3–4 physical links without synthesizing fake hardware data."

### Q3: How do you handle your original WiMotion model in WiMotion 2.0? Did you retrain it?
**Answer:**
> "We did **not** retrain or discard the original WiMotion model. The original model (`presence_model.pkl`, HistGradientBoosting) was trained on 217 temporal-spectral features (Doppler power spectrum, SVD spatial coherence, subcarrier variance) extracted from 1.0-second single-link CSI windows with 94.74% cross-validation accuracy.  
> In WiMotion 2.0, because we have an 8-link mesh, we built a dedicated **Link-Level Adapter (`V1PresenceModelAdapter`)**. This adapter groups multi-link packets by `(RX, TX)` pair, extracts the exact 217-feature vector per link, and evaluates presence independently per link before aggregating across the room. This preserves the original model contract completely."

### Q4: In your Replay Lab, is the data real or simulated?
**Answer:**
> "We maintain strict scientific provenance with zero fake data:
> 1. **Hardware Recorded (`benchmark_session_01`)**: Actual ESP32 multi-link hardware capture. All packet rates (~154 Hz), link variances, and RSSIs are computed directly from the recorded file timestamps using sliding-window binary search (`bisect`).
> 2. **Presentation Scenario (`demo_session`)**: A deterministic 95-second dual-person scenario specifically designed to synchronize with our recorded video demonstration on presentation screens. It is explicitly tagged with `source_type: "scripted_presentation"` in the metadata to ensure complete honesty.
> 3. **Public Dataset (`public_esp_fi_01`)**: Derived from the open-source ESP-Fi HAR benchmark with full license attribution.
> 4. **Synthetic Augmentation (`synthetic_session_01`)**: Generated by applying bounded RF gain fluctuation and Gaussian multipath perturbation to training data, tracking its parent session ID. It is never counted towards real hardware verification."

### Q5: What happens if an unmapped transmitter or rogue device transmits on the same channel?
**Answer:**
> "In `spatial/inference.py`, we explicitly validate transmitter IDs against our calibrated spatial map (`self.tx_zone_map`). If an unmapped or rogue transmitter transmits CSI packets, our system isolates it to `UNKNOWN` and **never** defaults it to a known zone. Furthermore, if aggregate packet rates drop below 4.0 Hz or valid packets in a window are under 8, our Signal Quality Gate triggers `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` or `SIGNAL_UNSTABLE`."

---

### Q6: What are the main limitations of the system?
**Answer:**
> "1. **Multi-path interference from static obstacles**: Metallic objects and concrete walls cause static reflections, which we mitigate using median baseline subtraction.
> 2. **LOS vs. NLOS sensitivity**: Non-Line-of-Sight human movement attenuates higher subcarrier frequencies more rapidly, which we capture using subcarrier-selective variance.
> 3. **Concurrency limits**: Precise continuous centroid localization for more than 2-3 overlapping targets in the same sector requires Angle-of-Arrival (AoA) antenna arrays, which is our proposed future extension."

---

### Q7: How does your system differentiate between the ML model and the spatial heuristic?
**Answer:**
> "We maintain complete separation of responsibilities:
> 1. **Stage 1 (ML Presence Gating)**: Driven by the original WiMotion model (`HistGradientBoostingClassifier`) using 217 temporal-spectral features (Doppler power, SVD spatial coherence, subcarrier variance). On untouched held-out hardware sessions, it achieves **77.98% accuracy (F1 = 83.1%)**.
> 2. **Stage 2 (Spatial Sector Localization)**: Driven by the `SpatialBaseline` algorithm based on Wi-CaL multi-link CSI variance ratio ranking ($argmax$). It determines whether perturbation is in Sector Z1, Z2, Z3, or Z4.
> We never conflate heuristic sector ranking with trained ML classification, and our evaluation suite benchmarks both independently with full confusion matrices."
